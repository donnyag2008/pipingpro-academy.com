    Test Pressure Formulae — ASME B31.3
    Hydrostatic test pressure
    PT = 1.5 × Pdesign × (ST/Sh) [barg]
    Pneumatic test pressure
    PT = 1.1 × Pdesign [barg]
    B31.4 / B31.8 (pipelines)
    PT = 1.25 × Pdesign [barg, typical]
    ST = allowable stress at test temperature
    Sh = allowable stress at design temperature
    (ST/Sh) capped at 6.5 — B31.3 §345.4.2; ratio applies to the hydrostatic case only
    Min. hold time 10 min (§345.2.2), then reduce to design pressure for inspection
    Pneumatic upper bound (B31.3 §345.5.4): PT shall not be less than 1.1× design, and shall not exceed the lesser of (a) 1.33× design, or (b) the pressure producing a stress > 90% SMYS at test temperature. No ST/Sh term applies to pneumatic piping (that term belongs to hydrotest, §345.4.2, and to ASME VIII Div.1 vessels, UG-100).
    Per SAES-A-004 §5.4 the required test pressure must be achieved at the lowest point of the system; account for the static head of the test liquid when checking the highest-rated and lowest-rated components.
    ISO / EN Standards Governing Pneumatic Test
    There is no single ISO standard dedicated to pneumatic testing with its own unique formula — the international framework works in layers. For process/plant piping the ISO code is a wrapper that adopts ASME B31.3, while the European code (EN 13480-5) is the most prescriptive equivalent and carries its own test-pressure rule.
    StandardScopePneumatic test pressure
    ISO 15649Petroleum & natural gas — plant piping. Adopts ASME B31.3 as its technical basis incl. inspection & testing.Per B31.3 §345.5.4 → PT = 1.1 × Pdesign; ≤ lesser of 1.33× or 90% SMYS
    EN 13480-5Metallic industrial piping — Part 5: Inspection & Testing (European code, PED route).Pneumatic = 1.1 × PS (max allowable pressure), then dropped to PS for inspection. Metal temp ≥ 25°C above brittle-fracture temp; enhanced NDT per Table 9.3.3-1
    ISO 13623Petroleum & natural gas — Pipeline transportation systems (cross-country pipelines).Strength test ≥ 1.25 × MAOP; hydrostatic preferred — gas/pneumatic only under restricted, risk-assessed conditions
    ASME VIII Div.1 (UG-100)Cross-reference — pressure vessels, not piping.PT = 1.1 × MAWP × LSR (the only place the stress-ratio term legitimately appears for pneumatic)
    Bottom line for B31.3 / ISO 15649 piping: the pneumatic test pressure is a flat 1.1 × design pressure — no ST/Sh ratio. EN 13480-5 uses the same 1.1 multiplier on PS. The stress-ratio multiplier is a Section VIII vessel concept and should not migrate onto piping.
    Hold Times — and the "1-hour" myth
    General hold times for Aramco plant piping & pipelines are governed by SAES-L-150 and SAES-A-004 Appendix F, not a single blanket figure. The widely-repeated "30 min → 1 hour" change is a misreading.
    CaseHoldSource
    Hydro — generalPer SAES-L-150 / App. F; field practice 30–60 min before walk-downSAES-A-004 App. F
    Hydro — externally primed/painted joints1 hour or 2× required hold, whichever greaterSAES-A-004 App. D(3)
    Pneumatic — step holds≥ 10 min at each stepApp. A(10/24)
    Pneumatic — final hold30 minApp. A(10)
    So the 1-hour minimum applies specifically to hydrotesting with externally primed/painted joints (and only when the service is not lethal / Category M). Don't apply it as a universal rule.

    Faster Testing — Isolation / Test Plugs
    Conventional open-ended pipe testing means welding on end caps, testing, then cutting them off and re-bevelling — with PWHT on alloy/CS. Mechanical test/isolation plugs grip and seal the bore instead, and are now explicitly sanctioned by SAES-A-004 §5.12.4. Vendor figures cite reductions on the order of 80–85% versus welded end-cap procedures, with the grip self-energising as test pressure rises.
    Vendor / toolUseNote
    Curtiss-Wright EST (GripTight)Open-end pipe & elbow test plugs; flange-weld test plugFlange test plug cited in ASME PCC-2 Art. 501; tests the flange-to-pipe weld in isolation (axial + hoop)
    STATS Group (pipe end plugs)Open-end strength/leak test; tie-in isolationME presence; double-block-and-bleed plugs allow hot work + tie-in test in one
    Reverse-integrity gasketLocal flange tightness in lieu of pre-start-up leak testSAES-A-004 §8: water / non-sour liquid HC only; not where NDT-in-lieu welds used
    Plugs are an Inspection-witnessed item: confirm pressure rating ≥ test pressure, follow vendor operating instructions, and tag/track per §5.12.4. They reduce schedule, not the duty of care.
    NDT in Lieu of Pressure Test — Closure Joints
    Where both hydro and pneumatic testing are impracticable, or for closure (golden) joints whose individual components were already pressure-tested during fabrication, NDT may substitute for the pressure test (SAES-A-004 §5.15, Appendix H). Approval is required before welding. Typical conditions in Appendix H: skilled welders (< 5% rejection), approved WPS, 100% RT of butt welds plus 100% PAUT of all welds, and visual inspection of each pass.
    B31.3 para 345.9 is the parent code provision for closure-weld exemption; SAES-A-004 §5.15.2 is how Aramco accepts it. For Category D fluid service, an initial service leak test per B31.3 345.7 may be used in lieu of the hydrotest (App. F item 9).
    Pre-Start-up Leak Test & Service Test — §8
    A pre-start-up leak test with inert gas at maximum operating pressure (then a service test at normal operating pressure) is required during commissioning for systems that are: Class 600 and above, in hydrogen service, or in sour service with H₂S > 0.1 mol% (1000 ppm). Liquid (B31.4) and water lines are exempt from the inert-gas leak test (service test only) — except sour service above the H₂S threshold.
    Water Quality, Contact Time & Lay-up — SAES-A-007 / SAEP-43
    The real schedule driver on Aramco jobs is often water chemistry and lay-up, not the pressure hold. Treated water needs O₂ scavenger to keep dissolved O₂ ≤ 20 ppb with residual scavenger ≥ 20 ppm, pH 6–9, TSS ≤ 50 ppm; flushing within 7 days of test.
    AssetMax water contact (Cat I, II-CDE, III)Cat II-A&B (e.g. 304/316, duplex)
    Plant piping & pressure vessels30 days3 days
    Heat exchangers & fin-fans14 days3 days
    Tanks90 days90 days
    If start-up will not occur within the allowable interval (SAES-A-007 Table 3: 30 d piping/PV Cat I; 14 d for Cat II-A&B and all exchangers; 90 d tanks), the system must be laid up. SAEP-43 defines four methods — wet (O₂ ≤ 20 ppb, scavenger ≥ 20 ppm, biocide, 15–30 psig positive pressure), dry (dewater → dry to dew point ≤ −1 °C / 30 °F), inert gas (displace under N₂ positive pressure), and ambient. Hot dry air as pressurisation medium is prohibited for Cat II-A&B materials and for sales-gas / NGL / sour pipelines.
    Valves with stainless-steel trim containing < 15% Cr must be removed before hydrotest (or specially dried with VCI per SAEP-43 §8.3.10/8.3.11). Mixing material categories in one test loop is restricted — e.g. Cat I must not be tested with Cat II-A&B.
    Pneumatic Test Limits — SAES-A-004 §5.8
    Water is the primary medium; pneumatic is only permitted after evaluating alternatives and obtaining approval, when hydro would damage linings/insulation, contaminate moisture-sensitive equipment, need significant support modification, or risk brittle fracture.
