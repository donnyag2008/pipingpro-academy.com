How This Calculation Works

The minimum required wall thickness for straight pipe under internal pressure is defined by the design equation in ASME B31.3 §304.1.2. This equation accounts for the pipe outside diameter, the internal design pressure, the material's allowable stress at design temperature, and de-rating factors for weld quality and high-temperature service.

t = PD / 2(SEW + PY)

P = internal design pressure (psig)
D = pipe outside diameter (in.)
S = allowable stress at design temperature (psi) — per B31.3 Table A-1
E = longitudinal weld joint quality factor — per B31.3 Table A-1A/A-1B
W = weld strength reduction factor — per B31.3 §302.3.5(e)
Y = coefficient from B31.3 Table 304.1.1

The calculated minimum thickness is then increased by the corrosion/erosion allowance and adjusted for the manufacturer's mill under-thickness tolerance — typically 12.5% for seamless pipe per ASTM standards:

tordered ≥ (t + c) / (1 − mill tolerance)

This calculator pulls allowable stress values from the PipingPro Academy materials database (based on ASME B31.3 Table A-1) and automatically applies the correct Y coefficient for your selected material and design temperature. Results include the next available standard pipe schedule per ASME B36.10M (carbon steel) or B36.19M (stainless steel).

Engineering note: For pipe with D/t ratio less than 6 (thick-wall pipe), the Boardman/Lame equation should be used instead of the simplified Barlow-based formula above. This calculator flags such cases automatically. For external pressure design, refer to ASME B31.3 §304.1.3 and the ASME BPVC Section II Part D charts.
