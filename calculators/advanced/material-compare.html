Reading a material comparison

A two-column property dump tells you little. What matters when you swap one material for another are the ratios and differentials that flow straight into wall thickness, flexibility, support loads and weld design.

1 · Hot allowable stress ratio — Sh,A/Sh,B at T

The headline for any substitution. Pressure design wall thickness is inversely proportional to S·E·W, so the allowable-stress ratio at the design temperature maps almost directly onto required wall and therefore weight and cost.

t = (P · D) / [ 2 (S·E·W + P·Y) ] (B31.3 §304.1.2)

Higher isn't always the winner: austenitic and Cr-Mo grades hold strength differently up the temperature scale, so the shape of the Sh–T curve matters as much as the ambient value. Watch where the curves cross.

2 · Displacement-stress allowable — SA

Flexibility headroom. The basic allowable stress range is built from both the cold and hot allowables:

SA = f (1.25 Sc + 0.25 Sh) (B31.3 §302.3.5)

The tool reports SA at f = 1. A material with a higher SA tolerates a larger expansion stress range before failing the flexibility check.

3 · Differential thermal expansion — Δα

For a material change in a connected run, or a dissimilar-metal joint, the expansion gap is the number you came for. Austenitic stainless expands roughly 30–40% more than carbon steel — real differential strain at a flange or weld between the two.

ΔL = α · L · (T − 21°C) | differential = (α_A − α_B) · L · ΔT
Dissimilar-metal joints: a CS-to-SS weld or flanged transition develops differential expansion proportional to Δα. This drives local thermal stress and, at bolted joints, gasket relaxation — size it, don't assume it away.

4 · Modulus ratio — EA/EB at T

Displacement (secondary) stress range scales with E. In a system mixing two stiffnesses, the stiffer material attracts more load. Two materials with identical α but different E still share thermal load unequally.

5 · Temperature envelope & data status

The comparison shows where both materials are code-listed versus where only one reaches the design point, and flags each material's data status — verified against the PipingPro dataset, or indicative pending confirmation against your code edition.

6 · Welding difficulty & relative cost

Two decision factors that never appear in a code table but often decide the outcome in practice. Welding difficulty reflects preheat/PWHT burden and procedure-control risk (e.g. sensitization in austenitics, sigma-phase risk in duplex, hydrogen cracking in Cr-Mo grades) — it is a practice-based indication, not a code requirement lookup; always confirm against your project WPS/PQR. The cost figure is a rough relative multiplier against carbon steel (A106B = 1.0), material cost only, no fabrication cost included — treat it as directional and confirm current mill pricing before using it in an estimate.

Source of truth: all values are interpolated by PPA.interp between the tabulated points in ppa-materials.js — the same dataset and algorithm the md datasheet uses, so the two pages cannot drift. Confirm the governing line against your project PMS before issuing.
