The §319.4.1(c) empirical check

ASME B31.3 §319.4.1 lets you skip a formal flexibility analysis when a system (a) duplicates one with a successful service record, (b) can be judged adequate by comparison with previously analysed systems, or (c) is a two-anchor system of uniform size with no intermediate restraints that satisfies the empirical relation, eq. (16):

D·y / (L − U)² ≤ K₁

where D = pipe OD, y = resultant of total displacement strains to be absorbed, L = developed length between anchors, U = straight-line anchor distance, and K₁ = 208 000·SA/Ea (SI, result in (mm/m)²) or 30·SA/Ea (US). SA is the allowable displacement stress range from eq. (1a); Ea is the reference modulus at 21 °C (70 °F).

Because the thermal growth and the anchor span share the same net offsets, y = e · U for uniform temperature, where e is the unit expansion rate — so the tool can derive y and U from the net ΔX/ΔY/ΔZ offsets and e, or take y, L, U directly.

Where eq. (16) can be unconservative

The code cautions that the relation is not a proof of adequacy and may understate stress for: unequal-leg U-bend geometries (large L/U), nearly straight "sawtooth" runs (L/U near 1), large-diameter thin-wall pipe, and cases where displacements perpendicular to the anchor line dominate. This tool flags those geometries even when the number passes.

Worked example (validation)

A DN 200 (NPS 8) Sch 40 carbon-steel L-shaped two-anchor run: a 7.62 m leg and a 3.66 m leg, design 93 °C, install 21 °C, e = 0.8 mm/m.

D = 219.1 mm
ΔY = 3.66 × 0.8 ≈ 3 mm · ΔZ = 7.62 × 0.8 ≈ 6 mm
y = √(3² + 6²) ≈ 7 mm
L = 3.66 + 7.62 = 11.28 m
U = √(3.66² + 7.62²) ≈ 8.5 m
D·y/(L−U)² = 219.1 × 7 / 2.78² ≈ 198 ≤ 208.3 → no formal analysis required

The calculator reproduces this case with the default values loaded.
