## WEIGHT LOADING RULES

### Items to include in weight loading:
- Pipes
- Valves and strainers
- Flanges and blinds
- Fittings
- Insulation material and covers (cladding)
- Fluid in the pipe — especially important for spring supported piping
- Others as necessary (snow, ice for exposed piping)

### Fluid Weight for Analysis

Unless otherwise specified, weight of fluid shall be taken as follows:

For LIQUID service: 100% of full water weight

For VAPOR, GAS and STEAM service:
- Up to 14" diameter: 10% of full water weight
- 16" and larger: 5% of full water weight

For FLARE HEADER: 
- Up to 10" diameter: 100% of full water weight
- 12" and larger: dead load of pipe only (no fluid weight)

When spring supports are used, the ACTUAL weight of fluid shall be taken as much as possible (not the simplified rules above).

### Pipe Wall Thickness for Weight Calculation

Unit weight of pipe included in computer program shall be input using "Nominal Wall Thickness" — no matter whether mill tolerance and corrosion allowance are considered in the analysis or not.

### Pipe Wall Retirement Thickness

When calculated pressure design thickness is less than retirement thickness, use the retirement thickness:

Size Range | Retirement Thickness
1/2" to 4" (50-100mm) | 0.06" (1.52mm)
6" to 12" (150-300mm) | 0.09" (2.29mm)
14" to 24" (350-600mm) | 0.12" (3.05mm)
26" to 46" (650-1150mm) | 0.15" (3.81mm)
48" and above (1200mm+) | 0.20" (5.08mm)

Minimum wall thickness shall not be thinner than:
- Alloy/Carbon Steel: 1/2"-2" = Sch 80; 3"-24" = Sch STD
- Stainless Steel: 1/2"-2" = Sch 80S; 3"-10" = Sch 40S; 12"-24" = Sch 10S

### Hydrotest Weight Consideration

If system is 14" diameter or larger, normally gas filled, and will be hydrotested:
- Establish resulting support loads
- Determine if temporary supports are needed
- Temporary supports may be required for spring-supported systems during hydrotest
