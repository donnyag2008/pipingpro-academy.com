## NOZZLE MOVEMENT CALCULATION METHODS

Operating temperature of equipment shall be used for nozzle movement calculations.

### 1. Column and Vertical Vessel
- Neutral point of expansion = equipment supporting level (base)
- Prepare elongation charts showing vertical movement at each nozzle point
- For equipment temperature >200°C: also consider circumferential direction movement of nozzle and support
- Vertical movement at nozzle height H: displacement = H x e (where e = unit thermal expansion at operating temp)
- Temperature at each level uses average of adjacent nozzle outlet temperatures: (T1+T2)/2, (T2+T3)/2, etc.

### 2. Pump, Steam Turbine and Centrifugal Compressor
- Use operating temperature (pump, turbine inlet, compressor operating temp) as calculation temperature
- Vertical movement: H x e (H = nozzle height above base)
- Horizontal movement: W x e (W = horizontal offset from centerline to nozzle)
- Where e = unit thermal expansion at operating temperature

### 3. Reciprocating Compressor
- Use compressor operating temperature as calculation temperature
- Movements calculated same as centrifugal: H x e vertical, F x e horizontal

### 4. Air-Fin Cooler (AFC)
- Use mean operating temperature of inlet and outlet as calculation temperature
- Header box movements:
  - Vertical: L3 x e (from fixed point at bottom)
  - Horizontal: L4 x e (lateral from fixed point)
  - Note 1: Bundle design shall accommodate movement due to piping thermal loads
  - Note 2: For specific occasions, fixed point may be established when required
- AFC tube bundle RESISTANCE (reaction force "R") due to piping force (thermal expansion + friction force "F"):
  - If R > F: Restraint condition of nozzle point may be taken as free point for displacement and rotation, friction force "F" shall be considered as nozzle reaction force
  - If R <= F: Restraint condition of nozzle point shall be taken as fixed point

### 5. Heat Exchanger (Shell and Tube type)
Case 1 (Same Service — both shells same fluid):
- Longitudinal: use mean operating temp of shell inlet and outlet x unit expansion
  - L1 x es for nozzles C&D, L2 x es for nozzles A,B,H&I
- Circumferential: use mean operating temp of tube OR shell inlet/outlet
  - Different e values (et for tube, es for shell) at different nozzle locations

Case 2 (Differential Service — different fluids shell/tube):
- More complex: nozzle movements depend on combined shell and tube temperatures
- Nozzle A: (H3 x e1) + (H4 x e4)
- Nozzle F&G: (E1 x H3) - (H6 x e2)
- Each nozzle has unique formula based on geometry and which fluid temperature applies

### 6. Horizontal Vessel
- Operating temperature of vessel used as calculation temperature
- Longitudinal movement: L x e for each nozzle from fixed/slide saddle
- Circumferential movement: H x e for each nozzle height above centerline

### 7. Reactor
- Mean operating temperature of inlet and outlet = calculation temperature
- Neutral point of expansion = shell supporting level

### 8. Tank
- Tank nozzle movements per specific tank rules (bulging, settlement, rotation from vendor)

### SKIRT EXPANSION (for vessels on skirts)
Average skirt temperature formula:
  T_skirt = (T_top - T_ambient) x F + T_ambient
Where F depends on insulation:
  K = 1.0 for firebrick insulated
  K = 1.6 for non-insulated
And F is a function of K, skirt height (h), and skirt wall thickness (t).

### AROUND COLUMNS AND VERTICAL VESSELS — MODELLING RULES

Hanging Down Line (pipe going down from nozzle):
- Calculate nozzle movement at Point A (top) and Point B (bottom of vertical run)
- YB = YA - (H x e) where H = vertical distance between nozzle and lower point
- Consider weight support and expansion loop if stress/nozzle loads not acceptable

Bottom Line (Elbow Nozzle at skirt opening):
- Nozzle neck shall be taken as fixed point
- For displacement at skirt opening: check Y and Z clearance against "C" (skirt opening dimension)
- If sqrt(Y^2 + Z^2) <= C: support condition at skirt opening may be free point to displacement and rotation
- If sqrt(Y^2 + Z^2) > C: skirt opening shall be taken as restraint point and displacement "C" shall be considered as forced displacement

Steam Out Condition:
- Piping stress analysis for occasional condition — take most severe of:
  Case 1: Both towers hot
  Case 2: Tower A hot, Tower B cold
  Case 3: Tower A cold, Tower B hot
