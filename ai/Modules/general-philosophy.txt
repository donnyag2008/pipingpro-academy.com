## GENERAL PHILOSOPHY AND WORKING METHODS

### Core Principles
The basic responsibility of the Stress Engineer is to ensure piping is routed and supported so that no damage occurs to the pipe or associated equipment due to thermal expansion/contraction, weight, pressure, wind, earthquake, pulsation, shock, settlement, etc.

Key principles that MUST guide every analysis:
- Only enter ESSENTIAL comments on calculation title pages. Extraneous information wastes time and may confuse.
- Identify WORST CASE operating conditions BEFORE running computer checks, then run these cases only.
- If worst case is not immediately obvious, run necessary cases but after review choose only the EXTREME cases for detailed review with a note explaining the conclusion.
- Review configurations visually/approximately PRIOR to computer calculations so obvious problems can be discussed with piping designers before embarking on a computer run.
- Inform Piping Design of any configurations that have been passed but have loads/stresses CLOSE to allowable values, and FREEZE the arrangement.
- Keep designs REALISTIC: Minimise use of "super" special supports requiring high tolerances or complex designs. If these are required, it tends to indicate a RE-ROUTE is necessary.
- Wherever possible, AVOID flexible connectors (bellows, expansion joints) and springs.
- Use of COLD SPRING should be kept to a minimum.
- Where temperatures appear high compared to other data, discuss with Systems/Process engineer BEFORE calculation — highlight the impact on piping/equipment if the temperature proves inaccurate.

### When to Use Computer Analysis vs Visual/Approximate
(Design temperatures used for these thresholds)

VISUAL or APPROXIMATE methods acceptable for:
- Lines of 4" NB (100mm) and under
- 6" NB under 155°C (311°F)
- 8" NB under 145°C (293°F)
- 10" NB under 130°C (266°F)
- 12" NB under 120°C (248°F)
- 14" NB under 100°C (212°F)
- 16" NB under 90°C (194°F)
Note: The above must also be above -45°C (-43°F). This list is a guide only — for special piping (e.g. lined pipe), computer calculations should always be considered.

COMPUTER CALCULATIONS required for:
- All critical lines not covered above
- Lines over 3" NB attached to sensitive equipment (pumps, compressors, turbines)

### Calculation Checking Categories
All calculations must be checked by another stress engineer before issue.

COMPLETE CHECK required for:
- Air fan piping
- Compressor piping
- Turbine piping
- Three-pump hookups at elevated temperatures
- All equipment where nozzle loads are above 75% of allowable
- All systems where stress range is above 75% of basic SA value
- All systems with design temperature ≥ 300°C

GENERAL CHECK required for:
- All other computer calculations not requiring complete check

GENERAL REVIEW required for:
- All non-computer calculations (approximate and visual review)
