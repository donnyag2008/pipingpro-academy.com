MINIMUM PIPE CLEARANCES
Source: Bechtel 3DG-P41B-00002-001, Rev 001 (Dec 2004)
Scope: Minimum clearance between pipes and between pipe and stationary objects

---

DEFINITION: Minimum clearance = distance between two outermost surfaces of objects (or outside surfaces of pipe insulation if insulated). Requirements assume piping is supported per the span tables.

THREE CLEARANCE CONDITIONS:

1. WEIGHT LOAD: Deflection limited to 1/4 inch in weight span tables. Clearance > 1/4 inch required — easily achieved.

2. THERMAL EXPANSION: For 100 ft long CS pipe at 350°F: expansion = 2.26 in. For SS: 3.20 in. Maintaining 4 inches clearance between outermost surfaces of two parallel pipes is adequate for thermal expansion.

3. SEISMIC (MOST STRINGENT): Varies by pipe size, schedule, and seismic category. Three sub-conditions:

   From stationary object: 1.0 × required minimum clearance from tables
   Between two same-size pipes: 1.414 × required minimum clearance from tables
   Between different sizes: SRSS of the two pipe sizes' minimum clearances

ADDITIONAL CLEARANCE NOTE:
If SC-III pipe displacements govern minimum clearances (rather than access clearance), it may be more cost effective to support using 2× weight spans instead of 3× weight spans to reduce seismic movement. Access and maintenance clearances may govern over seismic clearances.
