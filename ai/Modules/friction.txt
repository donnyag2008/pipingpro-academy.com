## FRICTION RULES

Friction at pipe supports and restraints is a transient condition — maximum during plant start-up due to thermal displacement, diminishing with time. The reverse occurs at shutdown. B31.3 does not address friction fully or allow any particular relaxation in code allowable values.

### Critical Rules
- Friction SHALL NOT be used to REDUCE applied loads
- Additional loads due to friction at supports, restraints and equipment nozzles shall be considered when, in the Stress Engineer's judgement, friction will have a significant effect
- NO CREDIT shall be taken for frictional loadings when they would result in reduced nozzle loads (if friction makes nozzle loads look LOWER, ignore it; if HIGHER, include it)
- Near nozzles, consider friction effects during both pipe expansion AND contraction
- For complex pipe geometry, closely inspect horizontal force equilibrium when friction is included

### Friction Coefficients
- Steel to Steel: μ = 0.3
- Polished Stainless Steel to PTFE (Teflon): μ = 0.1

### PTFE Slide Plates
- Use to reduce friction at restraints
- Installation: Teflon face must act on a stainless steel plate
- Must not become exposed to dirt during operation
