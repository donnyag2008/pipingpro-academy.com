## CRITICAL LINE SELECTION AND ANALYSIS CATEGORIES

### PIPING CATEGORIES (KBR Method — 5 Categories)

Category 1 (Most Critical): Special systems beyond normal code methods
- High pressure service exceeding ASME B16.5 Class 2500 rating
- Category M fluid
- Design metal temperature exceeds 1000°F (538°C)
- Lines designed for >22,000 cycles (fatigue)
- Response spectrum / dynamic analysis for earthquake
- Unrestrained metallic expansion joints
- Large diameter pipe (>48")
- Piping subject to thermal bowing
- Any piping that in analyst's judgement needs Category 1 treatment

Category 2: Large/complex systems needing additional consideration
- Ethylene transfer piping, refractory lined piping
- Static earthquake or wind analysis required
- Acoustic study required

Category 3: Lines needing formal analysis due to diameter/temperature or connection to sensitive equipment
Lines connected to LOAD SENSITIVE equipment:
- Reciprocating pumps/compressors, centrifugal compressors, turbines >=3" at operating temp >250°F (121°C)
- Non-reciprocating pumps: >=4" at >=300°F, or >=12" at >=250°F, or one size larger than nozzle at >=250°F
- Air-cooler piping

Lines connected to STRESS SENSITIVE equipment:
- ASME VIII Div 2 vessels
- Fired heaters or furnaces
- Aluminum equipment

Category 4: Lines connected to sensitive equipment not meeting Category 3 size/temperature thresholds
- Approximate methods acceptable (Kellogg, Grinnell, Tube Turn)

Category 5: Low/ambient temperature, small size
- Visual inspection only with guided cantilever check
- No formal documentation required

### WHEN IS COMPUTER ANALYSIS NEEDED vs VISUAL/APPROXIMATE

Visual or approximate methods acceptable for (design temperatures):
- All lines <=4" NB
- 6" NB under 155°C (311°F)
- 8" NB under 145°C (293°F)
- 10" NB under 130°C (266°F)
- 12" NB under 120°C (248°F)
- 14" NB under 100°C (212°F)
- 16" NB under 90°C (194°F)
Must also be above -45°C (-43°F). For special piping (e.g. lined), computer calculation always considered.

Computer calculations required for:
- All critical lines not covered above
- Lines over 3" NB attached to sensitive equipment

### FLEXIBILITY ANALYSIS GRADES (IKPT-KBR Method — Grades A, B, C)

Grade A — Review by Piping Engineer:
- Normally no stress analysis required
- Piping engineer reviews and may upgrade if judged necessary

Grade B — Simplified Method:
- Analysis using ANSI/ASME B31.3 formula 319.4.1
- Or simplified flexibility check form

Grade C — Detail Analysis:
- Full computer analysis per ASME B31.3 requirements

Classification charts determine grade based on:
- Chart 1: Lines NOT connected to rotating equipment or AFC — by pipe size and temperature
- Chart 2: Lines connected to rotating equipment — by pipe size and temperature (lower thresholds)
- Chart 3: Lines connected to tanks without flexible joints — by pipe size and temperature

Lines always classified Grade C regardless of size and temperature:
- High pressure (>20 barg) steam lines
- Flare lines subject to sudden temperature change >300°C
- All process, regeneration and decoking lines to/from fired heaters and steam generators
- All process lines to/from centrifugal compressors and blowers
- All steam lines to/from turbines
