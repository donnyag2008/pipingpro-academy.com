PIPE OFFSET LENGTH AND SUPPORT LOADS — THERMAL EXPANSION
Source: Bechtel 3DG-P41B-00002-001, Appendix 2 and Appendix 4 (SAMPLE TABLES)

---

PURPOSE: Offset lengths determine the minimum distance from a direction change (elbow/bend) to the first pipe guide or restraint, to absorb thermal expansion and keep stresses within Code allowable. Also used to determine acceptable distance for first seismic lateral support from equipment nozzle.

DESIGN MODEL: A pipe offset (L-bend) where the expansion length feeds thermal growth into the offset leg.

DESIGN RULES:
1. Tables based on expansion length = 10 ft. For expansion length X ft:
   New offset length = tabulated offset × (X/10)^0.5
   New support load = tabulated load × [(X/10) / (new offset length/10)^(1/3)]

2. If operating temperature is between two tabulated temperatures, use the higher temperature values.

3. Offset length in tables is the MINIMUM required to meet Code allowable.

4. Guide support loads at the offset may be critical for support design — the support allowable may require increasing the offset length to reduce the load (e.g. if offset end is a vessel nozzle, nozzle allowable governs).

5. For temperature < 100°F, thermal stress and loads are generally insignificant.

6. Offset length for thermal may conflict with seismic span requirements. Frequent communication between layout designer and stress engineer is necessary.

APPENDIX 4 — MINIMUM PIPE OFFSET LENGTH FOR BEND PIPES

Purpose: Quick-check tables that ensure the piping system simultaneously meets BOTH the seismic span requirement AND the thermal offset length requirement at the first support near the bend/elbow.

Available for: 100 ft, 80 ft, 60 ft, and 20 ft expansion lengths
Materials: CS and SS
Categories: SC-I and SC-II/III

Tables provide: 0.75 × Seismic Span (empty/water) alongside minimum offset lengths at 100°F through 350°F for each pipe size and schedule.

DESIGN PRINCIPLE: The offset length from the table must fit within the available 0.75 × seismic span. If the required offset exceeds 0.75 × seismic span, the designer must either:
- Increase span between supports (if Code allows)
- Add flexibility (loop, offset, expansion joint)
- Consult stress engineer for detailed analysis

The Young's Modulus used: 27.9E6 psi (CS), 28.3E6 psi (SS)
Thermal coefficients per ANSI B31.3 Code
Modified seismic span = 0.75 × span from Span Tables (accounts for bend/elbow effect)

SAMPLE — CS/SC-I, 100 ft expansion:
8 in Sch 40: 0.75×Seismic = 52.2/40.2 ft | Offset at 300°F = 17.5 ft → fits within seismic span
16 in Sch 10: 0.75×Seismic = 70.4/42.8 ft | Offset at 350°F = 26.5 ft → fits within seismic span (w/water governs)

Full tables for all 16 combinations (CS/SS × SC-I/SC-II,III × 100/80/60/20 ft) are available in the source document.
