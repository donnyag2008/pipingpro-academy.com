## EQUIPMENT-SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE: COMPRESSORS

### CENTRIFUGAL COMPRESSORS (API 617)

Nozzle Load Check Equations (API-617, Sixth Ed., Feb 1995):

Individual nozzle check:
  Fr + 1.09 x Mr <= 54.1 x De  (Metric SI: N, N.m, mm)
  3Fr + Mr <= 927 x De  (US Customary: lbs, ft-lbs, inches)

Where:
  Fr = sqrt(Fx^2 + Fy^2 + Fz^2) — resultant force
  Mr = sqrt(Mx^2 + My^2 + Mz^2) — resultant moment
  De = nominal pipe diameter for sizes <= 200mm (8")
  De = (400 + Dnom)/3 for sizes > 200mm (8") in mm
  De = (16 + Dnom)/3 for sizes > 8" in inches

Combined resultant check (inlet + sidestream + discharge):
  Fc + 1.64 x Mc <= 40.4 x Dc  (Metric SI)
  2Fc + Mc <= 462 x Dc  (US Customary)

Where Dc = equivalent pipe diameter of one circular opening equal to total area of all openings.
For Dc > 200mm: Dc = (460 + Equivalent diameter)/3

Individual component limits at each nozzle (Metric SI):
  Fx = 16.1 Dc, Fy = 40.5 Dc, Fz = 32.4 Dc
  Mx = 24.6 Dc, My = 12.3 Dc, Mz = 12.3 Dc

These values pertain to compressor structure only. Forces and moments in connecting pipes, flanges, and bolting must not exceed those allowed by applicable codes.

Loads may be increased by mutual agreement between purchaser and vendor, but expected operating loads must be minimized.

### CENTRIFUGAL COMPRESSOR SPECIAL REQUIREMENTS

1. Very careful investigation of each pipe line connected to the compressor — check supports.
2. Each line should be stopped at a location giving LEAST thermal expansion at that point. Stop location does not necessarily mean support point.
3. Field note on isometric drawing highlighting that the "stop" shall be connected to compressor nozzle.
4. First two supports nearest compressor nozzle should ALWAYS be spring supports — compensates for vertical thermal expansion and reduces time to connect pipe to nozzle.
5. Stops should be designed for minimum practical deflection — check combined deflection of stops and structural members they attach to in flexibility analysis.
6. Dead-weight loads on nozzles should be as close to ZERO as possible using constant spring supports (per NEMA SM-23 commentary). Variable springs acceptable if movement is small.
7. Spring variability around equipment should be kept as LOW as practical to minimise cold nozzle loads.

### RECIPROCATING COMPRESSORS (API 618)

Early discovery is critical — the later a reciprocating compressor is discovered on a job, the fewer changes can be made to limit vibration-prone piping, increasing cost.

PULSE BOTTLE SIZING:
- Pulsation suppression bottles per API-618 para 3.9.2.2 for suction (Vs) and discharge (Vd)
- Total cylinder displacement (one cylinder):
  Head end displacement = 0.785 x (bore)^2 x (stroke)
  Total displacement approx 1.18 x (gas volume per min) / (RPM x no. cylinders per stage)
- Major volume = (major volume factor) x (total cylinder displacement)

EXTENT OF VIBRATION:
- Pressure pulses travel from compressor through suction AND discharge until meeting a "stop point"
- All piping between compressor and stop points is "subject to vibration"
- Stop points: vessel with enough vapor volume (>= major volume), point where pressure drop equals/exceeds average line pressure upstream of orifice, blind or always-closed valve
- All BRANCHES from vibrating piping are also subject to vibration (including branch-of-branch)
- Exception: branch within fitting distance of a "major volume" vessel nozzle
- Liquid lines from vessel subject to vibration are NOT considered vibrating

PIPING DESIGN FOR VIBRATION CONTROL:
- Do not use unreinforced branch connections
- Eliminate as many bends as possible
- Avoid long overhangs and long unguided risers
- Group pipe lines together, run as close to grade as practical
- Trunnions for support can be same sizes as main pipes to increase thickness
- Cinch anchors shall NOT be used for anchoring or stopping pulsating lines
- Use adjustable type (wedge) supports for volume bottles located directly under the compressor

VIBRATION SUPPORT LOCATIONS:
- Every vibrating line must be restrained perpendicular to pipe longitudinal axis in BOTH directions
- Vibration supports provided for TWO POSITIONS past each stop point
- Place vibration supports at: heavy load locations, piping discontinuities (reducers, tees, branches), piping changes in direction (bends, elbows)
- Adjacent spans should NOT have identical frequencies — vary span lengths
- Line up vibration supports on parallel lines to reduce number of piers
- Anchors and stops at all support points where possible — must not interfere with thermal movements
- Pulsating lines should NEVER be stopped/anchored to building steel or platforms — use independent foundations

### ROTATING EQUIPMENT — GENERAL RULES

- Rotating equipment allowable loads are permissible for LONG TERM CONTINUOUS operation
- Compare calculated piping loads based on NORMAL OPERATING TEMPERATURE — not design temperature, not seismic, not short-term conditions
- Thermal and weight nozzle loads are compared to allowables from pump vendors or industry standards
- Field millwright provides proper alignment by adjusting support systems and/or localized heating to parallel flanges
- However, thermal alone AND weight alone must each meet nozzle load allowables
- Various load cases (steam-out, settlement) may use increased allowable nozzle limits — typically 1.5 or 2 times, as agreed by client
- Reaction loads calculated using COLD modulus of elasticity — may be reduced by ratio of hot/cold modulus to bring within allowable
- Systems satisfying rotating equipment nozzle loads at operating conditions will generally have pipe stresses less than 1/3 of code allowables — stress compliance check typically not required unless significant design or flexibility temperature variations exist
- Use of COLD SPRING for piping connected to rotating equipment is PROHIBITED

### STANDBY EQUIPMENT TEMPERATURE RULES (from IKPT-KBR)

For lines connected to two or more equipment with one spare:

Two pumps/turbines with one spare — 3 cases:
- Case 1: A-hot, B-hot (both operating)
- Case 2: A-hot, B-cold (A operating, B standby)
- Case 3: A-cold, B-hot (B operating, A standby)

Three pumps/turbines with one spare — 4 cases:
- Case 1: A-hot, B-hot, C-hot
- Case 2: A-hot, B-hot, C-cold
- Case 3: A-hot, B-cold, C-hot
- Case 4: A-cold, B-hot, C-hot

STANDBY PUMP TEMPERATURE:
- For lines with warm-up BY-PASS: standby pump temperature = 70% of operating temperature
- For INSULATED lines without bypass (TS-RUN): standby = 50% of operating temperature
- For NON-INSULATED lines without bypass (TS-RUN): standby = 25% of operating temperature
