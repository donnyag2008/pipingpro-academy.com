## THERMAL LOADS AND TEMPERATURE CONSIDERATIONS

### Elastic Modulus Rules
- Thermal LOADS: use elastic modulus at MAXIMUM temperature (hot lines) or MINIMUM temperature (cold lines)
- Thermal STRESSES: use elastic modulus at 21°C (70°F) for ALL hot lines, and at minimum temperature for cold lines
- Hot lines = primarily subject to thermal expansion; Cold lines = primarily subject to thermal contraction

### Flexibility Temperatures — All Types to Consider

a) DESIGN TEMPERATURE: Maximum temperature from line designation tables. Used for code stress compliance.

b) NORMAL OPERATING TEMPERATURE: From line designation tables. Generally NOT considered UNLESS adjacent pipe or equipment have higher differential operating temperatures than design temperatures.

c) SOLAR AND AMBIENT TEMPERATURES: Must be considered when calculating stress range. MUST be considered for applied loads if higher than Design temperature.
- Typical values: Ambient 3°C to 43°C, Installation 21°C, Solar 65°C (project-specific — verify)

d) STEAM-OUT: Establish steam-out temperatures and which lines will be steamed out with the Process/Facilities group.
- Consider if steam-out temperature exceeds design conditions
- Equipment loads for steam-out may be increased by up to 2x for short-term duration — confirm with equipment manufacturer
- Consider whether equipment and piping are steamed out TOGETHER or SEPARATELY

e) STEAM TRACING: Use steam tracing temperature for flexibility if greater than design temperature. Electrically traced lines use design temperature.

f) REGENERATION: Consider carefully — obtain accurate temperature, frequency and duration. Allowable stress range may need reduction per B31.3 Table 302.3.5 to avoid fatigue failure.

g) DECOKE: Extreme condition of short duration — must be considered in flexibility analysis where appropriate.

h) FIRE: Special consideration — design principle is that system may be over-strained but MUST NOT FAIL under fire conditions. After a fire, all affected piping would be replaced.

i) INTERNALLY LINED PIPE:
- Concrete-lined water piping: use Solar temperature for expansion. If buried or externally insulated, use ambient.
- Refractory-lined pipe: metal temperature significantly less than commodity temperature — get accurate skin temperature from Process group.

j) PIPE BOWING: When differential temperature exists around pipe circumference:
- Flare line subject to radiant heat on top
- LNG line at start-up (lower half cools before line fills and cools upper half)
- Get temperature differentials from Process group
- First estimate for LNG differential temperature: 30°C
