P&ID MTO Extraction Method

The AI reads each P&ID drawing in two stages: first a fast title-block scan to extract drawing number, area/unit, and revision; then a comprehensive MTO extraction identifying every pipe line, valve, instrument, equipment item, fitting, special item, and utility connection.

Pipe lengths are estimated from schematic line lengths using routing complexity factors: simple ×1.3, moderate ×1.7, complex ×2.2, skid ×3.0, rack/long-run ×1.3. These are AACE Class 4 estimates with ±30% accuracy.

Piping Estimation Method

Once MTO is extracted, the estimation engine applies industry-standard parametric norms to derive engineering deliverables and man-hours. The cascade follows: line count → isometrics (1.8/line) → spools (3.5/iso) → man-hours (28 MH/iso for design + 50 MH/stress package). Design man-hours are split across 3D modelling (22%), layout & design (18%), pipe support engineering (18%), isometric extraction (13%), MTO & line list (12%), specs & interface (7%), and checking/QA (10%).

Norms are based on AACE 18R-97 equipment-factored methodology, J.S. Page's Estimator's Piping Man-Hour Manual, and practitioner planning figures calibrated on Middle East gas plant and GOSP projects.
