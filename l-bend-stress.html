L-Bend Two-Anchor Stress

Thermal flexibility of an L-shaped pipe run fixed at both ends — displacement stress range, stress ratio, longitudinal & vertical elbow movement, and the force & moment at each anchor. Solved as a full planar-frame flexibility problem, not a guided-cantilever approximation.

Screening tool. Material property and allowable-stress values are indicative and must be verified against the governing edition of ASME B31.3 (Table A-1 / Appendix C) before use in design. Bending-flexibility model with a rigid corner — not a substitute for a full CAESAR II flexibility analysis.

Method

The L-bend is a statically indeterminate planar frame. One anchor (B) is released, the 3×3 bending-flexibility matrix [f] of the released cantilever is formed in closed form, and the released anchor is held to zero net displacement against the free thermal growth (the force / compatibility method). The anchor reactions then follow from equilibrium, and the internal bending moments give the displacement stress range.

[f] · {Fx, Fy, M}ᵀ = {−ΔL₁, −ΔL₂, 0}ᵀ
Free growth per leg: ΔL = α·ΔT·L. Solve for the restraint forces Fx, Fy and corner couple M, then evaluate the internal moments at A, the corner, and B.
SE = |M|max / Z
SA = f ( 1.25 Sc + 0.25 Sh )  — B31.3 §302.3.5
f = min(1.0, 6·N⁻⁰·²)  — stress-range reduction factor
Elbow movement (corner node):
δL (longitudinal, along Leg 1) = α·ΔT·L₁
δV (vertical, along Leg 2) = ( Mcorner·L₁²/2 + Fy·L₁³/3 ) / EI
Assumptions & limits

Bending flexibility only. Axial and shear flexibility are neglected (standard hand-calc simplification). The corner is a rigid welded joint — no elbow flexibility factor (k) or stress-intensification factor (i) is applied. Ignoring the SIF is non-conservative on local stress, partly offset by the conservatism of the rigid corner; apply the SIF separately for detailed work.

Modulus. The cold reference modulus Ea is used for the stress range per B31.3 §319.4.4.

Elbow movement. Longitudinal δL is the free axial growth of the horizontal run; vertical δV is the transverse bending deflection of Leg 1 under the restraint forces. Axial shortening under load is neglected, so for near-straight runs (very short second leg) the movement is an approximation. Assumes a vertical-plane bend; for a horizontal-plane bend read δL/δV as the two in-plane axes.

Material allowable-stress and expansion values are indicative and curated for screening. Always confirm against the governing ASME B31.3 edition before design use.
