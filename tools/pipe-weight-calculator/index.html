How This Calculation Works

Pipe weight per unit length is calculated from the pipe cross-sectional metal area and material density:

W = Am × ρ

Am = (π/4) × (D² − d²)

D = pipe outside diameter (in. or mm) — per ASME B36.10M / B36.19M
d = pipe inside diameter = D − 2t
t = nominal wall thickness for the selected schedule
ρ = material density (lb/in³ or kg/m³)

Outside diameters and nominal wall thicknesses are taken from ASME B36.10M (carbon and alloy steel pipe) and ASME B36.19M (stainless steel pipe). Material densities follow the values in Mohinder L. Nayyar's Piping Handbook — for example, carbon steel at 0.2836 lb/in³ (7,850 kg/m³) and Type 304/316 stainless steel at 0.289 lb/in³ (8,000 kg/m³).

The calculator also returns key section properties used in piping stress analysis and support span calculations:

I = (π/64) × (D⁴ − d⁴)   — moment of inertia
Z = 2I / D   — section modulus
Ai = (π/4) × d²   — internal flow area
Engineering note: Actual pipe weight may vary from calculated values due to manufacturer's mill tolerance on wall thickness (typically +/−12.5% for seamless pipe per ASTM standards). For weight-critical applications such as offshore lifts or pipe support load calculations, consider using the minimum or maximum tolerance-adjusted wall thickness as appropriate.
