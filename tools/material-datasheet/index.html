How This Tool Works

This tool retrieves the mechanical and physical properties for piping materials listed in ASME B31.3 Table A-1. Select a material specification and grade to generate a property datasheet showing:

S = basic allowable stress at each listed temperature (psi)
Sy = specified minimum yield strength (psi)
Su = specified minimum tensile strength (psi)
E = quality factor for longitudinal weld joints — Table A-1A / A-1B
P-No. = P-number grouping for welding qualification per ASME BPVC IX

Allowable stress values in B31.3 Table A-1 are derived from the lower of time-independent criteria (⅔ × Sy or ⅓ × Su at temperature) and, above the creep range, time-dependent criteria based on 100,000-hour rupture strength. The transition temperature where creep governs is indicated by italicised values in the ASME table — this tool flags those entries so the engineer can identify the creep regime at a glance.

The database covers 259 material specifications including carbon steel (A106, A53, API 5L), austenitic stainless steels (A312 TP304/316), duplex and super duplex (A790 S31803/S32750), and nickel alloys (B407, B444), with all values stored in US Customary units as published in the ASME code.

Engineering note: Always verify that the material specification listed on the mill certificate matches the grade, heat treatment condition, and product form assumed in the piping material class. B31.3 Table A-1 allowable stresses are specific to product form (pipe, plate, forging) and condition (annealed, normalised, solution-treated) — selecting the wrong line can yield unconservative stress values.
