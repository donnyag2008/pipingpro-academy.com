Initial Movement Calculation

Calculate the initial nozzle/support displacement due to equipment thermal growth — for input into CAESAR II or AutoPIPE stress analysis.

Engineering Note: Nozzle displacement calculations are industry best practice (not directly codified in B31.3-2024). Methods align with API 610 (pumps), API 650/653 (tanks), and ASME B31.3 flexibility requirements (§319). Always verify against project-specific equipment datasheets and vendor data.

Vertical Vessel — Nozzle Movements
Fixed point is the base support or skirt anchor bolt. Displacement is calculated section by section between consecutive nozzles. For each section: Tavg = (Tlower + Tupper) / 2, then α is looked up at Tavg, and section expansion = α × Lsection × (Tavg − Tinstall). The displacement at each nozzle is the cumulative sum of all section expansions below it. Each nozzle has its own shell temperature depending on the process stream.

Engineering Notes
Initial movement calculations per industry best practice and PipingPro Academy methodology. Thermal expansion coefficients (α) extracted from the PPA material database (ppa-materials.js), based on ASME B31.3 Table C-1 and Table C-3.
For preliminary engineering use only. Results must be verified against project-specific equipment vendor data and GA drawings.

Basic Theory
Δ = e(T) × L
e(T) = thermal expansion rate at temperature T (mm/m)
L = distance from fixed point to nozzle (m)

The thermal expansion rate e is extracted from the PipingPro Academy material database (ppa-materials.js), which is based on the Mean Coefficient of Thermal Expansion (α) from ASME B31.3, Table C-1 (Carbon & Alloy Steel) and Table C-3 (Austenitic Stainless Steel & Nickel Alloys).

The exact nozzle operating temperature is used to look up the expansion rate — no separate ΔT calculation is required. The expansion rate e(T) already represents the total thermal growth per unit length from the code reference temperature (70°F / 21°C) to temperature T.
When performing piping stress analysis — especially for lines connected to equipment nozzles — one of the most critical inputs is the initial nozzle movement due to equipment thermal growth. Equipment nozzle movements must be applied as displacement boundary conditions in CAESAR II or AutoPIPE, not simply as rigid fixed points. Normal operating temperature at each nozzle is used.
Material Database & Expansion Coefficients
The PipingPro Academy material database covers 10 material groups:
• Group 1 — Carbon & Low Alloy Steel (A106, A516, A105…)
• Group 2 — Low Alloy Cr-Mo (A335 P11/P22…)
• 5Cr-1Mo Steels (A335 P5…)
• 9Cr-1Mo Steels (A335 P9/P91…)
• Austenitic Stainless Steel (304, 316, 321, 347…)
• Ferritic / Duplex SS (12Cr–27Cr)
• Monel (67Ni-30Cu) N04400
• Inconel 600, 625 & Incoloy 800

Each group contains the Mean Coefficient of Thermal Expansion α (×10⁻⁶ in./in./°F) at multiple temperature points. The calculator interpolates α at the exact nozzle temperature and converts to the total expansion rate e in mm/m.
Source: ASME B31.3, Table C-1 and Table C-3. Implemented in ppa-materials.js — the shared material library used across all PipingPro Academy calculators, ensuring consistency.
Vertical Pressure Vessel
Nozzle Movement (section-by-section, cumulative):
ΔY = Σ [ e(Tavg,i) × Li ]

For each section between consecutive nozzles:
  Tavg,i = (Tlower + Tupper) / 2
  ei = expansion rate at Tavg,i (mm/m)
  Li = section length (m)

Fixed point = base support / skirt anchor bolt.
Each nozzle has its own shell temperature depending on the process stream at that elevation. The displacement at each nozzle is the cumulative sum of all section expansions below it.

Radial: ΔR = e(Tnozzle) × Rmean

Support Point (CNode): YB = YA − (e × H)
YA = nozzle movement, H = nozzle-to-support distance
e = expansion rate at shell temperature at the support location
For top nozzles, place the nearest support as close as possible to the nozzle. Since the support attaches to the vessel shell, it moves with the vessel — include via CNode in CAESAR II.
Horizontal Vessel
Longitudinal: Δlong = e(Tnozzle) × L
Circumferential: Δcirc = e(Tnozzle) × H

Fixed point = fixed saddle. L = distance along vessel axis from fixed saddle to nozzle. H = vertical distance from vessel centreline to nozzle centreline.
Each nozzle uses its own operating temperature to determine the expansion rate.
The fixed saddle is the datum. The sliding saddle allows the vessel to expand freely. L is measured along the vessel axis. H accounts for the circumferential (radial) growth from the centreline.
Pump
Δ = e(Tnozzle) × L in each axis (X, Y, Z)
Fixed point = pump baseplate / foundation anchor bolt
L = distance from anchor to nozzle flange face in each axis
Each nozzle (suction, discharge) uses its own operating temperature.
Dimensions from the vendor GA drawing. For hot-service pumps, suction and discharge temperatures may differ significantly — use the actual temperature at each nozzle.
Heat Exchanger
Longitudinal (shell nozzles): Δ = es × L
Longitudinal (tube nozzles): Δ = et × L
Circumferential: Δ = e(T) × H (from vessel CL)

es = expansion rate at mean shell temperature = (Tshell,in + Tshell,out) / 2
et = expansion rate at mean tube temperature = (Ttube,in + Ttube,out) / 2
Fixed point is typically the tube-sheet end (channel end) for fixed tube-sheet type.
Shell-side and tube-side have different temperatures and therefore different expansion rates. Differential expansion between shell and tubes should be considered for floating-head and U-tube exchangers.
Reciprocating Compressor
Δ = e(Tnozzle) × L in each axis (X, Y, Z)
Fixed point = foundation anchor bolt
Each nozzle uses its own operating temperature — suction and discharge temperatures differ significantly on compressors.
Compressor suction is typically much cooler than discharge. Always use the actual temperature at each nozzle, not a single average.
Air Fin Cooler (AFC)
Δ = e(Tnozzle) × L
Each nozzle uses its own operating temperature (inlet or outlet).
Fixed point per vendor drawing.
Note-1: Bundles design shall accommodate movement due to piping thermal loads. Note-2: For specific occasions, a fixed point may be established when required.
Tank
Thermal Radial Growth: Δradial = e(T) × R
Thermal Vertical Growth: Δvertical = e(T) × Hnozzle
Bulging: rotation angle (θ) and bulge distance (Δ) from tank vendor
Differential Settlement: minimum 10 mm (industry practice)
Three considerations for tank nozzle movement: (1) thermal growth from tank centreline and base — calculated using the expansion rate at tank operating temperature, (2) shell bulging under hydrostatic pressure (rotation angle + outward bulge from tank vendor data), and (3) differential settlement of 10 mm minimum for post-hydrotest foundation settlement. If expected settlement exceeds 10 mm, use actual value from Civil/Geotech Group.
