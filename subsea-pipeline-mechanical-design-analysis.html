How to Do a Subsea Pipeline Mechanical Design Analysis

A practical, code-traceable workflow for wall thickness, combined stress, elastic bend radius, on-bottom stability, collapse, propagation and upheaval buckling — demonstrated end to end on a real 18-inch buried export line.

PipingPro Academy ~16 min read ISO 13623 · DNV-ST-F101 · DNV-RP-F109 · OTC 6335

A subsea pipeline must be shown to survive every phase of its life — installation, hydrotest, operation and shutdown — against a defined set of limit states. This guide sets out the complete mechanical design workflow, explains why each check is performed, gives the governing equations from the recognised codes, and demonstrates every step with figures reproduced exactly from a real 18-inch liquid line running from the well-pad manifolds (WP-1, WP-2, WP-3) to a Central Processing Facility (CPF).

1Introduction and scope

The objective is not only to arrive at a set of numbers, but to leave you able to defend each result against the standard it derives from. A subsea pipeline mechanical design analysis assembles the following checks:

  1. Wall thickness for pressure containment (bursting), to ISO 13623:2017 and DNV-ST-F101.
  2. Combined (equivalent) stress in the fully restrained condition, using the Von Mises criterion to ISO 13623:2017.
  3. Minimum elastic bend radius derived from the available stress margin.
  4. On-bottom stability of the as-laid line, to DNV-RP-F109.
  5. Local buckling / external-pressure collapse, to DNV-ST-F101.
  6. Propagating buckle resistance and buckle-arrestor spacing, to DNV-ST-F101.
  7. Upheaval buckling of the buried line, by the simplified Palmer method of OTC 6335.

The first three checks govern the pressure-containing wall and the routing of a restrained line. On-bottom stability, collapse and propagation govern an unburied or as-laid pipe. Upheaval buckling governs a trenched-and-buried pipe operating hot. The worked line here is buried, so wall thickness, combined stress and upheaval buckling are the controlling deliverables.

Design philosophy

ISO 13623 applies an allowable-stress approach (a usage factor on yield). DNV-ST-F101 applies a limit-state, load-and-resistance-factored approach (partial safety factors γm and γSC). Both coexist in a typical subsea scope: the line-pipe wall and combined stress are taken to ISO 13623, while collapse and propagation — which depend on water depth — are taken to DNV-ST-F101. They are complementary, not interchangeable.

On selected wall thickness

The pressure-containment calculation sets the minimum wall; you then select the next standard schedule. For this line the combined-stress check uses t = 11.91 mm and the upheaval-buckling check uses the as-selected t = 14.91 mm. The worked examples retain each value so the figures reconcile exactly with the issued calculations.

4Step 1 — Wall thickness for pressure containment

Wall thickness is the foundation of the whole analysis. The wall must avoid three distinct failures: bursting (pressure containment), local buckling (collapse under external pressure), and propagating buckling. Pressure containment is sized first; collapse and propagation are then verified, and buckle arrestors are added rather than thickening the wall, because using propagation to set the wall is uneconomic.

Governing equations

The hoop stress from pressure containment (ISO 13623:2017 Cl. 6.4.1.1) and the pressure design thickness are:

σhp = SMYS · fh · ft
t = Do (Pid − Pod) / [ 2·σhp + (Pid − Pod) ]

The nominal wall is the pressure thickness plus corrosion allowance and mill tolerance, and the diameter-to-thickness ratio is checked against the DEP limit:

tnom ≥ t + CA + mill tolerance   |   D / t ≤ 60

Worked example — 18″ line

Substituting SMYS = 415 MPa, fh = 0.77, ft = 1.0, Pid = 15 MPa, Pod = 0:

QuantityComputationResult
Allowable hoop stress σhp415 × 0.77 × 1.0319.6 MPa
Pressure design thickness t457.2 × 15 / (2×319.6 + 15)10.49 mm
Selected nominal wall tnomnext schedule ≥ t + CA11.91 mm
D/t ratio457.2 / 11.9138.4 ≤ 60 ✓
Result

Minimum pressure-containment thickness is 10.49 mm. The next standard schedule gives tnom = 11.91 mm, and D/t = 38.4 satisfies the DEP limit of 60. This selected wall is carried into the combined-stress check.

5Step 2 — Combined equivalent stress

A buried (or otherwise axially restrained) pipe cannot expand freely when pressurised and heated, so it develops a longitudinal stress that combines with the hoop stress. The Von Mises equivalent stress captures this interaction and is compared with an allowable derived from yield.

Governing equations

The hoop stress, the two longitudinal components, and their sum:

σhp = (Pid − Pod)(Do − t) / (2t)
σL = μ · σhp   |   σEA = E · α · (Tc − T)   |   σT = σL + σEA

The Von Mises combined equivalent stress (ISO 13623:2017 Cl. 6.4.1.2; torsion neglected for a buried line) must not exceed the allowable:

σeq = √( σhp² − σhp·σT + σT² ) ≤ feq · ft · SMYS

The thermal term σEA is negative (compressive) because operating temperature exceeds installation temperature; the pressure term σL is tensile. feq = 0.90 for the functional-plus-environmental combination. For the corroded case the wall is taken as t − CA.

Worked example — 18″ line

Using Do = 457.2 mm, t = 11.91 mm, Pid = 15 MPa, μ = 0.30, E = 203,000 MPa, α = 1.17×10⁻⁵/°C, (Tc − T) = −60 °C, feq = 0.90:

QuantityComputationResult
Hoop stress σhp15 × (457.2−11.91) / (2×11.91)280.41 MPa
Longitudinal pressure σL0.30 × 280.4184.12 MPa
Longitudinal thermal σEA203000 × 1.17e-5 × (−60)−142.51 MPa
Total longitudinal σT84.12 + (−142.51)−58.38 MPa
Von Mises σeq√(280.41² − 280.41×(−58.38) + 58.38²)313.70 MPa
Allowable feq·ft·SMYS0.90 × 1.0 × 415373.50 MPa
Utilisation313.70 / 373.5084.0 % ✓
Result

The Von Mises equivalent stress is 313.70 MPa against an allowable of 373.50 MPa — a utilisation of 84.0 %. The restrained line PASSES. The remaining 16 % margin is what makes elastic bends possible, and is used directly in Step 3.

6Step 3 — Minimum elastic bend radius

Where the route changes direction and space allows, the pipeline is routed by elastic bending rather than fittings. The bend adds a bending stress that must fit inside the stress margin left over from the combined-stress check, so the minimum radius comes directly from that margin.

Governing equations

Sb1 = feq · ft · SMYS − σeq
Rcur = E · Do / (2 · Sb1)

where Sb1 is the maximum stress available for bending (MPa) and Rcur the minimum elastic bend radius (m, with E in MPa and Do in mm). A smaller margin forces a larger — gentler — minimum radius.

Worked example — 18″ line

QuantityComputationResult
Stress margin for bending Sb1373.50 − 313.7059.80 MPa
Minimum elastic bend radius Rcur203000 × 457.2 / (2 × 59.80) / 1000776.0 m
Result

The line can be elastically bent to a minimum radius of 776 m without exceeding the Von Mises allowable. Any field bend tighter than this needs a fitting or a re-check of the combined stress with the bending term included.

7Step 4 — On-bottom stability (as-laid line)

Before a line is trenched or buried it rests on the seabed, where it must not float or be displaced. To DNV-RP-F109, vertical stability requires the submerged weight to exceed the buoyancy with a margin:

γw · b / ws ≤ 1.0   |   b = ρw · g · π · D² / 4

where γw is the stability safety factor (1.1 if a low probability of negative buoyancy is not demonstrated), b the buoyancy per unit length, ws the submerged weight per unit length, D the outside diameter including coatings, and ρw the seawater density (1025 kg/m³). Where the bare-steel line is too light, submerged weight is added through a concrete weight coating until the criterion is met — and that weight then contributes to the upheaval-buckling resistance once the line is buried.

8Step 5 — Local buckling / external-pressure collapse

In deeper water the external hydrostatic pressure can collapse the pipe at its weakest (most ovalised) cross-section. To DNV-ST-F101 the net external pressure must stay below the characteristic collapse pressure reduced by the partial safety factors:

Pe − Pmin ≤ Pc / (γm · γSC)

The characteristic collapse pressure Pc is the root of the DNV collapse cubic, which couples elastic and plastic collapse with the initial ovality:

[Pc − Pel]·[Pc² − Pp²] = Pc · Pel · Pp · f0 · D/t
Pel = 2E (t1/D)³ / (1 − ν²)   |   Pp = fy · αfab · 2t1/D

where Pel is the elastic collapse pressure, Pp the plastic collapse pressure, f0 the initial ovality (1.5 % here), αfab the fabrication factor (0.85 UOE), and t1 = t − tfab the characteristic wall thickness using the fabrication tolerance (≈ 1.0 mm). Pmin is the minimum internal pressure that can be sustained — usually zero for an as-laid (empty) line. The collapse requirement can drive a thicker wall in deep water, so it is run at the maximum installation/empty water depth.

9Step 6 — Propagating buckle and arrestors

If a local buckle or dent initiates, it can run — propagate — along the line under external pressure, since the pressure needed to propagate a buckle is only 15–20 % of that needed to initiate collapse. The propagating-buckle check to DNV-ST-F101 is:

Pe ≤ Ppr / (γm · γSC)   |   Ppr = 35 · fy · αfab · (t/D)2.5

Designing the full wall to resist propagation is uncommonly expensive and over-conservative. The economic solution is to accept that a buckle could propagate and to limit how far it travels by installing buckle arrestors at intervals along the line. The arrestor raises the local bending stiffness so a running buckle is stopped (the cross-over pressure Px), and its required length follows from the propagating capacity of the adjacent pipe and of an infinite arrestor — keeping any single event to one joint between arrestors.

10Step 7 — Upheaval buckling (buried line)

Once the line is trenched and buried, the failure mode of concern changes. A buried pipe operating hot and pressurised cannot expand axially, so it builds up a large compressive driving force. At an over-bend (a vertical imperfection left by the trench profile) that force tends to lift the pipe upward against its own weight, its bending stiffness and the resistance of the soil cover. If the available download is too small, the pipe breaks cover — an upheaval buckle. The simplified Palmer method of OTC 6335 confirms the depth of cover is adequate.

The driving force and section properties

The effective expansion (driving) load combines the pressure end-load, the tensile Poisson component and the compressive thermal-restraint component:

As = π/4 (D² − d²)  |  Ai = π/4 · d²  |  I = π/64 (D⁴ − d⁴)  |  EI = E·I
Tel = (Pi·Ai − μ·σh·As) + α·E·As·(T2 − T1)

where d = D − 2t is the bore, As the steel area, Ai the bore area, σh = Pi·D/2t the hoop stress, and EI the flexural rigidity.

Download required and uplift resistance

The download per unit length required to prevent upheaval (OTC 6335, Eq. 12) is a function of the driving force, the imperfection height δ and the flexural rigidity:

Wr = [ 1.16 − 4.76·√(EI·Wte/δ) / Tel ] · Tel · √(δ·Wte/EI)

The soil provides uplift resistance per unit length (OTC 6335, Eq. 13), evaluated for both uncompacted and compacted backfill:

q = H · D · ρb · g · ( 1 + f·H/D )

where Wte is the empty-pipe weight, δ the imperfection height (mm), H the depth of cover, D the diameter over coatings, ρb the backfill density, and f the uplift coefficient (0.1 uncompacted, 0.5 compacted). The line is stable when the total available download Q = Wto + q exceeds the required download Wr.

Worked example — 18″ line

With D = 457.2 mm, t = 14.91 mm, Pi = 15 MPa, (T2−T1) = 60 °C, H = 0.8 m, δ = 300 mm, 3 mm FBE coating, contents 1025.5 kg/m³ and backfill 1850 kg/m³ (g = 9.80665 m/s²):

Result

The required download to prevent upheaval is 1,521 N/m. The available download — operating weight plus soil uplift resistance — is 10,962 N/m (uncompacted) and 15,606 N/m (compacted), both far in excess of the requirement. At 0.8 m of cover the line has no propensity for upheaval buckling.

Method note

This is the simplified Palmer (OTC 6335) screen using the exact Poisson term and backfill mass density. A fuller treatment — DNV-RP-F110 (Peters) allowable-imperfection, Schaminée / Pedersen peak uplift, the Cambridge mobilisation data and the tri-linear soil model — is warranted where the simplified screen is marginal.

11Putting it together — the analysis workflow

The seven steps form a single dependent chain. Wall thickness is sized first; the selected wall feeds the combined-stress check; the stress margin from that check sets the elastic bend radius. In parallel, the as-laid line is verified for on-bottom stability, collapse and propagation against water depth; and the buried line is verified for upheaval buckling against depth of cover.

StepCheckStandardWorked result
1Wall thicknessISO 13623 / F101tnom = 11.91 mm; D/t = 38.4 ✓
2Combined stressISO 13623 Von Mises84.0 % utilisation ✓
3Elastic bend radiusISO 13623 (Von Mises)Rcur = 776 m
4On-bottom stabilityDNV-RP-F109add concrete weight if light
5Local collapseDNV-ST-F101verify at max water depth
6Propagating buckleDNV-ST-F101arrestors set spacing
7Upheaval bucklingOTC 6335 (Palmer)no propensity at 0.8 m cover ✓

A check that fails does not necessarily mean a thicker wall. On-bottom stability is solved with weight coating; propagation with arrestors; upheaval with more cover. Reserving wall thickness for pressure containment, collapse and combined stress keeps the design economic.
